# Amoeba Game — Map

## Destination

A lean, decisions-first design spec — not a full GDD — sufficient to unblock building a playable vertical-slice prototype: the core grow/absorb loop, the two signature levels (the whale level and the gravity-planet level), and enough of the 5-class amoeba roster and 8-ability roster to prove the "every level solvable multiple ways" premise actually holds up in play.

## Decisions so far

- [Confirm engine & tooling](issues/01-engine-tooling-choice.md) and [scaffold](issues/02-scaffold-project.md): Godot 4.7, project in `game/`, run with `godot --path game`.
- Core premise: single-player puzzle-action, Spore Cell Stage-like — grow by absorbing smaller Amoebas, must be bigger than an Orb to Absorb it (the goal). Hand-authored levels, explorable map 4-8x the visible screen.
- Level philosophy: every level must be solvable multiple ways (e.g. eat-everything vs. outsmart-bigger-predators-to-reach-orb). Player picks a 2-Ability Loadout from a roster of 8 before entering a level; the level must remain solvable via most Loadout combinations, each in its own way.
- Ability/Ammo visual language: the player's Ammo/Ability inventory visually orbits the character; more Ammo widens the orbit. The player's size and art visually evolve as it grows.
- Destruction economy: when an Amoeba Shatters, it produces Fragments shaped like a smaller-scale echo of the original creature; Fragments serve as both Food and Ammo.
- Art direction: translucent, cell-under-a-microscope look, where creatures share/bleed a bit of the light value of their surroundings; light-toned player vs. darker-toned enemies for readability; abstract hybrid of "underwater" and "in space" mood, not literally either; creature movement/silhouette should be recognizable-but-abstracted (evokes a real animal without literally copying it — krill schooling and fleeing on whale-detection is the reference example).
- Concept art (stylistic reference): [paramecium under darkfield microscopy](concept-art/01-paramecium-microscope.webp) — deep teal field, thin luminous rim-lit membrane, translucent body with visible internal organelles in warm amber/orange against cool teal, fine cilia fringe and speckled texture. Style target only, not a creature design.
- More concept art (same microscopy family, darker/moodier navy-black palette): [dark navy paramecium](concept-art/02-paramecium-dark-navy.webp), [dense cilia texture](concept-art/03-dense-cilia-texture.webp), [membrane edge crop](concept-art/04-membrane-edge-crop.png), [heart-shaped vacuole](concept-art/05-paramecium-heart-vacuole.webp), [budding/dividing cell](concept-art/06-paramecium-budding.webp). Shared traits: near-black blue field with a soft halo around the cell, bright thin membrane line, stippled/streaky cytoplasm texture from fine cilia, round amber-ringed organelles (some with spiky halos) as the warm accent, one dark large nucleus-like shape as the focal contrast, and bubbles/debris in the surrounding medium. Several are video screenshots with UI overlays (mute/zoom icons, black bar) that should be ignored.
- Whale silhouette: the whale-Class Amoeba is modeled on *Bursaria truncatella* — a large, bag/cup-shaped ciliate with a deep funnel-like oral cavity along its body, known for swallowing other ciliates (e.g. paramecia) whole. Fits the whale level's "get swallowed to reach the Orb" mechanic and the recognizable-but-abstracted rule; the open mouth cavity is a natural interior for the Orb. Reference: [Bursaria photo](concept-art/07-bursaria-reference.webp) — a pale, translucent bag with a huge flared mouth at the front-bottom fringed with cilia, a curved band of ciliary rows running along the inner body, swallowed prey (small paramecia and vacuoles) visible inside, and small paramecia swimming near the mouth for scale (a ready-made whale-and-krill read). Note: this is a light, grey-background brightfield image with a caption overlay, so only the silhouette and anatomy carry over, not the palette. Details feed [Design the whale level in full](issues/09-whale-level-design.md) and [Finalize the class roster](issues/04-class-roster.md).
- Not everything is edible: some predators in a level stay predators for that whole level — "eat everything" is never guaranteed to be available, and a level can deliberately include threats the player must evade or exploit rather than consume. Open: whether this means "you never outgrow it within this level" or "inedible regardless of size" — to settle in [Define win-condition variants](issues/06-win-conditions.md) and [Define core growth & size-comparison rules](issues/03-growth-size-rules.md).
- Same creature, different role across levels (design principle): a Class's threat level is relative to the player's size at that point in the campaign, so the same creature can be terror in one level and a food source a few levels later. Reference encounter: coral larvae.
- Coral larvae encounter (level idea): first seen as what looks like a solid, static grape-vine-like shaft with dozens of small creatures attached. After a short moment (a few seconds) they dislodge, notice the player, and swarm toward them — a jump-scare reveal that the "object" was alive. Reference: [coral spawning still](concept-art/08-coral-spawning-reference.webp) — an orange-and-cream branching shaft densely studded with small polyps on a near-black background, with loose orange particles drifting around it. That uniform, regular, bumpy texture is what makes the "solid object" read, and the drifting orange specks are the early hint that something is detaching. It is a screenshot of a third-party photographer's social post (Brooke Pyke Photography), so use it as private reference only, not as an in-game or published asset. Several levels later, once the player is much bigger, the same larvae are a food source from the start. Depends on a campaign with growth carrying across levels, which is not yet decided (see Not yet specified).
- Stages (prototype, provisional): a new stage each time the player grows 20%, tied to which creature class becomes edible; stage-ups trigger a slow-motion zoom pull-back cutscene and the camera zoom shrinks per stage, so everything reads smaller as you grow. Launch also opens with a zoom-out intro. Details in [the prototype ticket](issues/11-core-loop-prototype.md).
- Audio/feel direction: player actions (Absorbing, using an Ability) should land with satisfying, musical feedback — the reference is the tactile pleasure of "caressing a piano," i.e. discrete, pitched, almost-melodic notes per action rather than generic impact SFX.
- Signature level 1 ("whale level"): named as fog, not yet ticketed in detail — awaits [Design the whale level in full](issues/09-whale-level-design.md).
- Signature level 2 ("gravity-planet level"): named as fog, not yet ticketed in detail — awaits [Design the gravity-planet level in full](issues/10-gravity-planet-level-design.md).
- [Research comparable games](issues/08-research-comparable-games.md): Osmos/agar.io/Spore/Feeding Frenzy point to color-coded size threat + inverse speed-to-size as core balance levers; krill/whale biology suggests a telegraphed cooldown-gated lunge predator for the whale level; orbital-mechanics precedents (Gravitar, Solar 2, Osmos) all require a trajectory-preview aid for the gravity-planet level. Full findings in [research/comparable-games.md](research/comparable-games.md).

## Not yet specified

- Broader level roster beyond the two signature levels, and any meta-progression / level-select structure between levels. New wrinkle: the coral-larvae "terror now, food later" idea implies the player's size/Class standing persists or escalates across levels, whereas the current design (grow within a level, start small each level) does not. Decide whether levels share a size baseline, or whether each level's starting size is authored to simulate the earlier/later feel.
- Whether the vertical slice needs any narrative/framing wrapper at all, or is purely mechanical.
- Solo-dev art/audio production approach in detail (tools, whether any outsourcing) — touched by ticket 01 but may need its own pass once engine is chosen.
- Difficulty/failure-state philosophy across levels beyond the two specific "devour" risks already named (whale level, gravity level) — e.g. is there a lives/retry system, permadeath, checkpointing.
- Full audio direction beyond the "satisfying, piano-like notes on action" principle — music, ambient soundscape, whether pitch/notes are diegetic or purely feedback.
